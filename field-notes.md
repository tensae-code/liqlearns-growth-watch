## Teachers & learning science on reaching students
- **OpenEd (2026)** — homeschool platform's honest AI-tutor assessment, written for parents: "AI tutors won't solve the 2 Sigma Problem on their own — Bloom's original research showed that human tutoring worked because of the relationship, not only the personalization." Plus the split: "AI excels at patient, consistent practice and instant feedback. Humans excel at motivation, relationship, and teaching reasoning. Best results combine both." Source: https://opened.co/blog/ai-tutors-homeschool — Why it matters: direct third-party validation of the user's core positioning (tutor-guided, not just AI); potential TikTok topic — "why AI tutors fail is relationships, not smarts."
- **Angela Watson** — former classroom teacher, host of the Truth for Teachers podcast — on reaching reluctant learners: "If I have an internal barrier in a student and I don't know how to handle it, I'm going for help... I am ordering books, I am talking to professionals." And the tactic: "if I'm struggling with a particular kid, I find somebody in the building who has had success with that kid and I say, 'Okay, tell me your secret'... I just shadow them: I study them, I become a sponge until I learn it." (She used this to reach undocumented students with multi-year schooling gaps after ESL, by partnering with a community liaison who sat with the families.) Source: https://truthforteachers.com/truth-for-teachers-podcast/motivating-disengaged-students/ — Why it matters: the tutor version of "go fix the human gap before blaming the learner" — LiqLearns' human-verification/support loop should copy this humility; strong TikTok framing for his Sunday outreach: ask, don't assume.
- **David Ginsburg** — 20+ years as a math teacher, instructional leader and PD provider (Philadelphia, via EdWeek) — redirecting students with "I and why": "What matters most is that students experience you as caring, not controlling" — e.g. "'I'd like you to take notes so you can refer to them during the lab' than 'write this down if you don't want to lose points.'" Source: https://www.edweek.org/teaching-learning/opinion-redirect-student-behavior-with-i-and-why/2014/01 — Why it matters: LiqLearns' AI coach "Liq" and its battle rules speak to kids constantly — caring-not-controlling language should be the product's default voice; every punitive system message is a retention risk.
- **EdWeek teacher panel** — classroom-teacher respondents on disengaged students ("Response: Several Ways to Connect With Disengaged Students") — the core insight: "Students who put up the façade of boredom or apathy are usually disengaging for good reasons, so it's up to us to keep working with them to find ways to get them fired up and excited about learning." Tactics: purposeful work (a hallway fraction display beats a worksheet), collaborative work, small choices, making it fun. Source: https://www.edweek.org/teaching-learning/opinion-response-several-ways-to-connect-with-disengaged-students/2012/05 — Why it matters: disengagement is information, not defiance — LiqLearns' analytics should treat "stopped showing up" as a lesson-design signal, not a learner failure; the choice/power angle also validates battles over worksheets.
- **Sue Larkey** — special-education/executive function practitioner working directly with children (Australia) — "The Last Three Puzzle Pieces": "I described starting a puzzle for a child by doing the first five pieces myself and leaving them the last three to five, because many of my kids don't know where to begin with a whole puzzle in front of them." Source: https://suelarkey.com.au/executive-function-skills-children/ — Why it matters: the hardest part of learning is starting — LiqLearns lessons should never open on a blank shell (the "No Content Yet" known issue proves why); scaffold the first steps so the learner only finishes, and TikTok gets a perfect metaphor: "do the first five pieces."

## New this run (2026-10-07 13:47 EDT — rotation 13 (teaching & learning science))
- **Paul Kirschner (educational psychologist, kirschner-ED)** — summarizing research on teachers' math anxiety: "Many students will spend a year, sometimes several years, learning maths from teachers who are tense about whether their students will meet the standard." The anxiety "travels through the room" — emotional leakage ("They sniff your fear... children learn that it is something you survive and not something you explore and enjoy"), narrowed instructional choices, and it's often a system response (accountability structures, pacing demands) rather than a personal quirk. Source: https://www.kirschnered.nl/2025/12/31/when-teachers-fear-math-students-feel-it/ — Why it matters: the GUIDE's confidence is the learner's climate — a tutor who flinches at fractions infects the student. LiqLearns vetting should screen for subject confidence, not just subject knowledge, and tutor training should address anxiety explicitly. TikTok topic: "your tutor's fear becomes your kid's fear."
- **Nan McCormack (retired teacher, now math specialist tutoring online, Chicago)** — on teachers who quietly avoid math: "I have some kids who say, 'Nan, we haven't done math for two weeks'... It's one of those subjects that teachers like to avoid." Her counter: deep content knowledge lets the teacher "draw out what's happening in a child's play around math... and know those trajectories, know the math inside and out so that you can be that expert when you're with the child." Source: http://hechingerreport.org/teachers-conquering-their-math-anxiety/ — Why it matters: this is the "guide until understood" job spec — the guide must know the subject deeply enough to spot teachable moments in what the learner is ALREADY doing. - **Eddie Brummelman (University of Amsterdam, via EdWeek)** — Dutch classroom study of 200+ grades 4–6 students: after a self-talk exercise, students who coached themselves on effort ("I will do my very best!") improved on the second half of a math test, while students who coached themselves on ability ("I am very good at this!") showed no gain: "our study found that the math performance of children with low self-confidence benefits when they tell themselves that they will make an effort." Source: https://www.edweek.org/leadership/anxiety-can-make-kids-avoid-math-heres-how-they-can-talk-through-the-fear/2019/12 — Why it matters: a concrete coach line — Liq (the AI coach) should prompt effort-self-talk before hard tasks ("tell yourself you'll do your best"), never ability-affirmations. Effort language is also the brand voice for battling through difficulty.
- **Kim Wooden (The 74)** — on responsive teaching, via second-grade teacher Mrs. Johnson: "she knew where every one of her students stood... gauged their understanding based on who answered a question immediately and who hesitated... identifying the groups of students who were struggling while making it all look effortless." The practice: "noticing what a student needs and responding in the learning moment" — "teacher judgment is the engine behind responsive instruction." Source: https://goodmenproject.com/featured-content/responsive-teaching-has-never-been-more-important-or-harder/ — Why it matters: notice-and-respond in the moment IS the LiqLearns brand ("a tutor guides you to succeed") — this is the spec for both human tutors and the AI coach: read hesitation as data, adjust mid-lesson, and treat judgment in the moment as the product's core skill.

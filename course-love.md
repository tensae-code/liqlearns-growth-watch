# Course love — why people LOVE a course (brand fuel)

The COURSE side of the watch. Not complaints — the opposite: why people love a course, a creator, a teacher. Instructor charisma, content craft, format, community, outcomes, marketing that trends. LiqLearns is building its BRAND around what people love, not just fixing what they hate.

The user's brand lens (his own words): the platform is a tool — the brand is "a tutor guides you to succeed, not just teach you and leave you be." Every course should FEEL like being guided through, not lectured at.

Entry format: who/creator, what people love about them (quote), link, what LiqLearns should steal.

- **NetworkChuck (Chuck Keith)** — the exemplar. What people love: "for a highly caffeinated view of pretty much all things server / network related... entertaining and informative" (Lawrence Systems forum); "Channels like NetworkChuck make hacking fun and accessible... Beginner-friendly labs" (Ethical Hacking Institute roundup). The formula: high energy + beginner-first + real hands-on labs, never dry lectures. Sources: https://forums.lawrencesystems.com/t/help-with-subnetting/15396 and https://www.ethicalhackinginstitute.com/blog/what-are-the-best-websites-to-learn-ethical-hacking-for-free — What to steal: cast LiqLearns tutors for enthusiasm first, credentials second; every lesson ships with a do-it-now lab, not just a watch-me video.
- **The Cioara principle (Jeremy Cioara, Chuck's CBT Nuggets mentor — the root of Chuck's style)** — "you can tell when somebody really loves what they're talking about." Cioara's signature came from one CBT Nuggets rule: no PowerPoint slides — so he taught on a whiteboard/drawing pad, with playful touches like his kids' Lego figures in frame. Chuck adopted the same genuine-enthusiasm doctrine. Source: https://github.com/claes-work/networkchuck-clone/blob/HEAD/wiki/sources/2025-07-28-yt-2jgDUaF8E8M.md (fan wiki transcribing Chuck's interview) — What to steal: enthusiasm is the medium. LiqLearns courses must be made by people who visibly love the subject; ban slide-reading delivery — show, draw, demo, do.
- **NetworkChuck's AI-security teaching** — "Using AI to become a Hacker": 7 ways to use AI for cybersecurity study — personalized study plans, AI-generated flashcards, AI quizzing on complex topics — plus free Notion templates and a GitHub prompt pack so viewers can copy the system. Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3D6gaawXwfk — What to steal: this IS the "guide you to succeed" model — AI as study coach, not content dispenser. And the giveaway templates are a lead magnet: free tools → followers → paid Academy.
- **The NetworkChuck funnel (the marketing engine the user described)** — free YouTube videos → Discord community → "study with me" Twitch streams → NetworkChuck Academy (paid). Every video description routes viewers down the funnel. People follow for the promotion style and energy; the follower count becomes the social proof that sells the course. Source: video description funnel, e.g. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mJ_5qeqGOaI — What to steal: the exact playbook — free high-energy content (TikTok/YouTube) → community → LiqLearns. The trend IS the ad.

- **David Malan / CS50 (Harvard, free on edX)** — enthusiasm as pedagogy, not decoration. What people love: "the infectious enthusiasm and masterful teaching of David Malan. Malan has a gift for making complex concepts feel approachable and even fun. His lectures are peppered with interactive demonstrations, colorful analogies, and self-deprecating humor... physical props help make abstract ideas tangible and sticky" — e.g. ripping a phone book in half to teach binary search, a can of Pringles for a stack. Live "slow reveal" coding where he codes gradually while narrating his thought process. Source: https://www.bomberbot.com/cs50/introduction-to-computer-science-a-deep-dive-into-harvards-legendary-cs50-course/ — What to steal: the props-and-demos formula — abstract topics need a physical anchor; LiqLearns tutors should show-and-do, narrate mistakes live, never just read slides. (Reinforces the Cioara principle: enthusiasm is the medium.)

What people love: student tribute: "Can you imagine staying glued to your screen for 2 hours straight, watching a Computer Science lecture rather than Netflix?... There's a type of bond developed with David... a sense of kinship with the community... You end up seeking help from strangers, solving their doubts, motivating them and even congratulating them on their achievements. This man's a magician, and his magic is teaching." Source: https://gist.github.com/wahabshaikh/20630a430f00c52e2b2a6653e2236b05 — Plus Prof. Margo Seltzer (Harvard): "He turned CS50 into a class with a real community"; a student: "If there were ever a cult, it's CS50." Source: http://www.thecrimson.com/article/2011/5/26/students-cs50-computer-cs/ — What to steal: build kinship, not just cohorts — learners who help and celebrate each other self-retain. Malan's course mantra also belongs on every LiqLearns lesson page: "what ultimately matters in this course is not so much where you end up relative to your classmates but where you end up relative to yourself when you began."

Malan: "If you get what we're doing here, you can easily translate it to other things... You can go out and learn anything else." Source: https://novemberlearning.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/MiddleSchoolScienceFairstotheIvoryTowerLessonsfromHarvardProfessorDavidJ.Malan_.pdf — What to steal: every LiqLearns course ends with a public showcase (battles already do this — make graduation a visible event); student projects that exceed the curriculum are the best ads and the best proof of "guided to succeed."
